Section 01 · Basics

What is a Mutual Fund?

Think of a mutual fund as a shared pot of money. Thousands of investors pool their savings together, and a professional fund manager invests that money in a basket of stocks on their behalf. You don't need to pick individual companies — the fund manager does that work for you.

When those stocks grow in value, your share of the pot grows too. When you invest in a mutual fund, you buy units — small slices of the overall fund. The price per unit is called the NAV (Net Asset Value), and it changes every market day based on how the underlying stocks perform.

🪣

Pooled Money

Many investors contribute small amounts. Together, this becomes a large corpus that can be invested in many companies at once.

👨‍💼

Professional Management

A qualified fund manager and research team handle stock selection, portfolio monitoring and rebalancing — you don't need to do anything.

📦

Diversification

Your money is spread across 30–80 companies. If one company falls, it doesn't hurt your overall investment heavily.

💧

Liquidity

Unlike fixed deposits or property, most mutual funds let you withdraw your money any business day. There's no lock-in (unless it's an ELSS fund).

📈

SIP — Invest Small, Grow Big

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) lets you invest a fixed amount every month — as low as ₹100 — automatically. Small amounts, compounded over years, build real wealth.

🛡️

SEBI Regulated

All Indian mutual funds are regulated by SEBI (Securities & Exchange Board of India), providing investor protection and transparency.

Section 02 · Shariah Compliance

Why Choose a Shariah-Compliant Mutual Fund?

A conventional mutual fund may invest in banks, liquor companies, insurance firms, or other industries that are impermissible under Islamic law. A Shariah-compliant fund applies a strict filter — called a Shariah screen — before selecting any stock.

Business Activity Filter

Companies are excluded if they earn revenue from alcohol, tobacco, pork, gambling, adult entertainment, conventional banking, insurance, or weapons manufacturing.

Debt Ratio Filter (Riba)

Companies with high levels of interest-bearing debt are excluded. Usually, interest-bearing debt must be less than 25–33% of total assets or market cap.

Revenue Purity Filter

Even if a company mainly does permissible business, impure revenue (interest income, rental from prohibited businesses) must be a small fraction of total revenue.

Ongoing Shariah Advisory

An independent Shariah Advisor or board reviews and updates the approved stock universe regularly. Stocks that fail compliance are removed from the portfolio.

Purification

If any minimal impure income does enter the fund (due to incidental interest), a small proportion of dividends may need to be donated to charity — a process known as purification.

Section 06 · Getting Started

How to Invest from the Gulf (UAE, KSA, Kuwait, Qatar…)

As an NRI (Non-Resident Indian), you are permitted to invest in Indian mutual funds — but there are a few steps you need to complete first. Here is a simplified roadmap:

01

Get an NRE or NRO Bank Account

You need an NRE (repatriable) or NRO (non-repatriable) bank account with an Indian bank. This is the source of your investment funds.

02

Complete KYC

Indian mutual fund regulations require KYC (Know Your Customer) verification. This can often be done online using your Indian passport, PAN card, and address proof.

03

Choose Your Platform

You can invest directly through the fund house's website (e.g., tatamutualfund.com), or via platforms like Groww, CAMS, or MF Central which support NRI accounts.

04

Start Your SIP

Set up a Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) — even ₹500–₹1,000/month is a meaningful start. Consistency over time is more important than the amount.

05

Monitor & Hold Long-Term

Equity mutual funds work best over 5–10+ years. Review your portfolio once or twice a year — avoid reacting to short-term market moves.

06

Note: USA/Canada Residents

NRIs resident in the USA or Canada face additional FATCA restrictions and many Indian AMCs do not accept investments from those countries. Gulf-based NRIs generally face no such restriction.
